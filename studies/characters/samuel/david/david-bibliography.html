How This Bibliography Works

The David study is Project Context's adaptation of BibleProject's teaching on David, drawn mainly from its podcast series and videos. The pages support that teaching with the Hebrew text, Second Temple writings and published scholarship. This page gathers every source the study uses, and the label under each entry names the page that uses it.

  • BibleProject Sources: the podcast episodes and videos the pages draw on (12 entries)
  • Scripture & Ancient Texts: the Hebrew Bible edition cited, the Second Temple texts quoted and one later Jewish text (5 entries)
  • Scholarship Cited in the Study: commentaries, literary studies, theology, articles and reference works (40 entries)
  • Formation, Leadership & Worship: the reading list from the Themes & Application page (7 entries)

Scripture & Ancient Texts

The Hebrew text, the Second Temple texts quoted and a later Targum

Hebrew Bible

Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia. Stuttgart: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 1997.
Identity Literary

Second Temple Texts Quoted

Psalms of Solomon 17. Jewish psalm, mid-first century BCE, praying for a righteous king, the son of David.
Second Temple Quoted: 17:21–23, 29–30, 32–33
4Q174 (4QFlorilegium). Dead Sea Scroll from Qumran that interprets 2 Samuel 7:11–14 alongside other texts.
Second Temple Quoted: frg. 1, col. 1, lines 10–13
Sirach (Ben Sira) 47:1–11. Praise of David in a wisdom book of about 180 BCE.
Second Temple Quoted: 47:2, 8–11

Later Jewish Reception

Targum of Psalms. Aramaic rendering of the Psalms; its surviving form is later than the Second Temple period.
Second Temple Discussed: Psalms 2:7 and 110

Method & Citation Notes

  • Main teaching influence: BibleProject's teaching is the main influence on the study's reading of David. The pages adapt and summarize it, and where a page follows one episode closely, that page names the episode.
  • What is listed: Only works that a page cites or recommends by name. Usage notes and page ranges stay on the page that cites the work.
  • Ancient texts: The Second Temple Period page quotes its texts in English without naming an edition, so none is listed here.
  • Citation format: Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition.
