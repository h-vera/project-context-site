Cover line two and the song collapses. The resurrection is the line the other five hang on.

Pair one

Who he is

Revealed in flesh. The incarnation. God did not send Ephesus a message; he arrived in a body. The Genesis decision kept: he would rather be human with us than be without us.

Vindicated in spirit. The resurrection. The world's verdict was a cross; God's verdict was an empty tomb.

Pair two

Who bears witness

Seen by angels. Heaven recognized the risen Christ before the world did.

Proclaimed in the nations. The announcement went out to the Gentile world, to cities exactly like Ephesus.

Pair three

Where he is trusted, where he reigns

Believed on in the world. Real people in real cities put their whole weight on him.

Taken up in glory. Ascended, enthroned, and the one who will appear again.

How we get there

From the garden to a six-line song.

Genesis

God makes a space, plants a garden in Eden, a name that means delight, fills it with life and puts us in his presence. With him we learn what it means to be human.

He decided long ago that he does not want his existence to be without us. There is no scenario in which God chooses to be without us.

Ephesus

Artemis was the city's system: her hope (a safe birth), her economy (the bank of Asia), her security (asylum), her ladder (status).

"Great is Artemis of the Ephesians" was the city's operating system (Acts 19).

The church

Some imported the same system with Jesus' name on it: controversy (1:4, 6:4), godliness as a way to make money (6:5), the love of money that pierces (6:10), faith shipwrecked (1:19), rules as a ladder (4:3).

The song

In the middle of a practical letter, Paul stops instructing and starts singing who Jesus is. Same first word as the city's chant, a different name: great is the mystery of godliness.

Paul builds the letter on it

The framework comes first; the letter is crafted around it. Where each pair echoes across 1 Timothy.

Pair one

  • 1:15 Christ Jesus came into the world to rescue and restore sinners
  • 2:5 one mediator, the man Christ Jesus
  • 4:4 everything God created is good (against the rule-makers of 4:3)

Pair two

Who bears witness

  • 2:7 Paul appointed herald and teacher of the nations
  • 5:21 before God, Christ Jesus and the elect angels
  • 3:7 good testimony with outsiders
  • 6:12 the good confession before many witnesses

Pair three

Where he is trusted, where he reigns

  • 1:16 a pattern for all who would believe on him for eternal life
  • 4:10 hope set on the living God, Savior of all
  • 6:14-16 until the appearing; unapproachable light
  • 6:17-19 hope not on riches but on God

2. Before Ephesus, Genesis

Henry1.5 min

Start at the beginning. In Genesis, God makes a space, and inside it he plants a garden in Eden, a name that means delight. He fills it with life, and he puts humans in the middle of it, in his own presence. That is where we learn what it means to be human. Not surviving. Living in the fullness of our existence, with him.

And when the humans walked away, God did not revise the plan. He had decided, long before Genesis 3, that he does not want his existence to be without us in it. There is no scenario in which God chooses to be without us. The whole story, Tanakh and New Covenant, is that decision working itself out. And the clearest place you can see the decision is in who Jesus is.

Hold onto that, because the song we are going to read is where that commitment gets sung.

3. The city of Artemis

Henry2 min

Now, Ephesus. The temple of Artemis was one of the seven wonders of the ancient world, and the whole city lived in its shadow. Artemis was not a hobby for the Ephesians. She was the system.

She was their hope: childbirth was the most dangerous thing most women would ever do, and Artemis was the one you called on to come through it alive. She was their economy: pilgrims, festivals, the silversmiths' shrine trade, and the temple itself was the bank of Asia. She was their security: her precinct was a place of asylum, where you ran when you were in trouble. And she was their ladder: serving her cult and funding her festivals was how a family's name rose in the city.

Hope, money, safety, status. "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians" was not a slogan. It was the city's operating system.

Now walk into the church. Some people inside the household of God had imported the same system and put Jesus' name on it. Paul names it. Controversies and word-battles, because controversy draws a crowd (1:4, 6:4). Godliness treated as a way to make money (6:5). The love of money that pierces people through with many pains (6:10). Faith shipwrecked (1:19). A new ladder built out of rules: forbid marriage, ban certain foods (4:3).

So Paul writes Timothy a letter, and most of it is practical. How to pray, who leads, how to care for widows, what to do with money. And then, in the middle of all that instruction, Paul stops instructing and starts singing. Before we get to the song, Holli is going to share.

5. The song: who Jesus is

Henry3 min

Here is 1 Timothy 3:16 as I render it, in sense lines:

And, in a manner confessed by all, great is the mystery of godliness:

Who was revealed in flesh,

was vindicated in spirit,

was seen by angels,

was proclaimed in the nations,

was believed on in the world,

was taken up in glory.

Everything today hangs on who Jesus is in these six lines, so look closely.

First, the frame. "Mystery" in Paul's mouth is not a puzzle. It is something God kept hidden and has now put on display. And every verb is passive. Revealed, vindicated, seen, proclaimed, believed on, taken up. Jesus is never the subject of a verb. God is doing all of it to him. This is not a resume of Jesus' achievements. It is a confession of what the Father did with the Son.

Now the three pairs. Every pair holds earth and heaven together.

Pair one, flesh and spirit: who he is. "Revealed in flesh" is the incarnation. God did not send Ephesus a message. He arrived in a body. That is the Genesis decision kept: God would rather be human with us than be without us. Artemis never came. Jesus did. "Vindicated in spirit" is the resurrection. The verb is Paul's justification word, but here it means a verdict. The world passed judgment on Jesus in the flesh, and the sentence was a cross. God overturned it in the Spirit, and the ruling was an empty tomb. Earth, then heaven.

Pair two, angels and nations: who bears witness to him. "Seen by angels": heaven recognized the risen Christ before the world did. "Proclaimed in the nations": the announcement went out to the Gentile world, to cities exactly like Ephesus. Heaven, then earth. The witness runs the full height of creation.

Pair three, world and glory: where he is trusted and where he reigns. "Believed on in the world": real people in real cities put their whole weight on him. "Taken up in glory": the ascension, the enthronement, the one who will appear again. Earth, then heaven.

Look at the pattern. Earth then heaven, heaven then earth, earth then heaven. The song zigzags between the two realms, and the thread stitching them together is Jesus. Artemis was the bridge between the gods and your childbirth, your money, your safety. This song says the bridge has a name, and he has already crossed.

And the hinge is line two. Cover it with your thumb and read the rest. Angels see a corpse. There is nothing to proclaim, nothing to believe on, no one to take up. The resurrection is not one line among six. It is the line the other five hang on.

6. Paul builds the letter on it

Henry1 min

This is what convinced me the song is not decoration. The framework comes first, and Paul crafts the letter around it.

Pair one, who he is: 1:15, Christ Jesus came into the world to rescue and restore sinners, and 2:5, one mediator, the man Christ Jesus. Pair two, who bears witness: 2:7, Paul appointed a herald and a teacher of the nations, and 5:21, a charge before God, Christ Jesus and the elect angels. Pair three, where he is trusted and reigns: 1:16, a pattern for all who would believe on him for eternal life, and 6:14, keep the commandment until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ.

The prayers, the qualifications, the widows, the money: Paul builds a congregation's whole life on who Jesus is. That is the move we have to make.

7. Shape our lives on it

Henry2 min

Here is the invitation. Look at your life this week: your challenges, the way you have been handling them, the people you are trying to help. Then take time, and take thought, and shape each one around who Jesus is, with the three pairs as the frame.

Pair one asks: who is Jesus in this? Is he here, in the flesh of this situation? And whose verdict am I living under, his or the world's? The resurrection says the verdict is already in.

Pair two asks: who is watching, and what am I proclaiming? The way you handle a hard thing is a sermon. Heaven sees it, and the nations hear it: your neighbors, your coworkers, your kids. You are always proclaiming something. The question is what.

Pair three asks: where is my hope resting? On what I can control, the money, the plan, the approval, the Artemis of the moment? Or on the one believed on in the world and taken up in glory, whose story already has its ending?

One more thing, because of who is in this room. We are people spread across a large country, with different cultures, in a society that makes money and gains power by dividing us: by region, by color, by class, by party. Artemis divided Ephesus too, into those who could afford her and those who could not. The song moves the other way. Proclaimed in the nations. Believed on in the world. Jesus is the one who unites every human being to God, across every country, every culture, and every century. No one is united to God through a flag, a party, or a bank account. We are united through the one who was revealed in flesh and vindicated in spirit.

That is why the resurrection is everything. It is not a doctrine at the edge of life. It is where we experience life. Our lives are enveloped in this song. Holli is going to share what that has looked like for her.
